# The Fen of Bellwater: Story Bible (Revised)

Oct 22, 2026 · @Makenna Linsky

## The world

**The Fen of Bellwater** is a wide marsh where folk and fae are said to live side by side. Its lanterns hold the **Fenlight**, light woven from the first wisps. The town believes the Fenlight keeps the **Murk** asleep beneath the water: an old fog that eats colour, warmth and memory.

**What the Murk really is.** The Murk is not hungry. It is full. Bogs don't destroy what falls into them; they preserve it, like bog bodies and peat-buried wood. The Murk is everything the fen has forgotten, stored in the deep water: lost names, faded songs, grief nobody wanted to carry. The Fenlight never killed it. It pushed forgetting downward, so the town above could stay bright and untroubled. Now, however, the memories needed to sustain the town's bright life are running out, so the murk can't be ignored for much longer.

**The bargain beneath the town.** "Side by side" is a pleasant lie. Folk live *above* the light. The fae of the **Drowned Court** live *beneath* it, and for generations they have absorbed what the town pushed down. They are half-swallowed because they have been the fen's landfill.

**What carrying the forgetting means.** Forgotten things don't vanish; they sink. Everything the town lets go of drifts down through the water and settles in the Court's halls. The drowned fae soak it up the way peat soaks up rain.

A drowned fae slowly fills with strangers' memories: grief for a dog they never owned, a song from a wedding they never attended. In exchange, they lose their own. The oldest courtiers can no longer tell which memories are theirs. The final stage is dissolving completely into the Murkbound. A bog-wight is what is left of a fae who forgot their own name.

The Court survives through rites. Courtiers wear masks carved with their own faces so they remember them, and speak their true names aloud at every gathering. Nobles hoard scraps of Fenlight, which slows the drowning. That is why the nobility has lasted while common fae have not.

**What the Fenlight is.** The first wisps were not light. They were memories, given up willingly by the first folk and fae who settled the fen, and woven into flame by **Mother Sedge**. Every lantern since has burned on that first gift, and the gift is running out. That is why the lanterns were failing.

**Rules of the light.** The Fenlight can hold the Murk back, but it can't destroy it. It can't be made by force, only from memory freely given. Every lantern lit draws on the same shared source.

## The player: the Surfaced

The player is a fae who climbs out of the Murk at the edge of Bellwater with no memory of who they are. The town calls them **the Surfaced**. Nobody recognises them, but Wren's last light flares whenever they come near, and nobody can explain why.

**The secret.** The player is a Drowned Court fae, and they were Linnet's guide. Linnet could never have found the Drowned Chapel alone. A young courtier who wanted the Court freed from the bargain showed her the way and helped carry the flames. When the seal closed, the guide was caught at its edge. The Murk took their memories, and they lay in the deep for seven years until the cracking seal let them go. Wren never met the guide, because Linnet kept them secret.

**Why the player stays.** Wren is the first person the player meets, and she takes them in that night. Her last light flared at them, and it hasn't changed in seven years, so she believes the player is connected to Linnet and could help her reach the Chapel. She can't say that without revealing her secret, so she offers practical reasons instead: a bed, work at a shop, a town that needs hands. For the player, Wren's light is the only clue to who they are. Both stay for reasons they don't say out loud, and that tension carries their friendship until chapter 3.

**The player's name.** The player chooses their own name, gender and pronouns at the start. The Court knew them by another, which Hemlock uses in chapter 3.

**Why this works in play.** The player's own memories are scattered through the Murk as memory fragments. Every fragment recovered on expeditions restores a piece of their past, so the player uncovers the mystery and their own identity at the same time. Regent Hemlock recognises them on sight and keeps it to himself until chapter 3.

**Why they remember across cycles.** Something that has already been through the Murk can't be forgotten by it twice. This is the in-world reason the player keeps knowledge between runs (see Endings and cycles).

## The Lantern Thief

**Seven years ago** a masked figure took every lantern in one night, and the Murk rose. Everyone assumes greed or malice. The truth, revealed across chapters 2–3: the Thief is **Linnet Alder**, Wren's younger sister.

**Why she did it.** Linnet found Lamplighter records proving the lanterns were failing, and that the order had known for years and hidden it. She knew they would stop her if she asked. So she took the flames in one night and carried them to the **Drowned Chapel**, where she used them to seal the Murk's heart. She is trapped inside the seal.

**Wren's lie.** Wren found the records first. The plan was hers. On the night, she lost her nerve, and Linnet went in her place. Worse, the Murk ate the town's memory of Linnet. Wren is the only one who still remembers she had a sister, because she kept the last light. For seven years she has pretended she never had one, because the truth sounds mad and makes her the coward.

**Why now.** The last light Wren kept has started to dim on a rhythm, like breathing. Some nights it shows a face in the flame. The seal is cracking, and Linnet is reaching out through the only light still connected to her. The crack also lets something out: the player.

**The caches.** Linnet needed only the flames, not the lanterns. She hid the empty lanterns across the deep fen as a trail for Wren. Each cache holds something only Wren would understand: a childhood code, a pressed flower, a half-finished letter. The player pieces together Linnet's reasoning before the chapter 3 reveal.

**Linnet before chapter 4.** Linnet is the heart of the story, so the player meets her long before the Chapel, in three ways:

- **Wren's flashbacks:** Wren's heart scenes play as memories of the sisters growing up: Linnet teaching Wren the cache code, the two of them carving masks. The night Wren lost her nerve is her last heart scene, available only after chapter 3.
- **Premonitions:** near caches and in thick fog, a ghostly Linnet appears at the edge of sight, repeating something she did seven years ago. Only the player sees her clearly; Wren sees only her light flicker.
- **The player's own fragments:** some of the player's memory fragments are of Linnet: her hand in theirs, her voice asking if they're nearly there, her laughing at something the player can't remember saying.

By chapter 4, the player should feel they know her, and miss her, before they have spoken to her.

**The cost of the seal.** Linnet is not simply a hero. Her seal holds the Murk's heart, but it also starves the fen of what the Murk stores. That is part of why colour keeps draining from Bellwater. Seven years inside has changed her too. She is half full of other people's forgotten things, and she may not want to be rescued.

**The tension in play.** Every lantern the player relights in town draws on the same Fenlight that holds Linnet's seal. Rebuilding the town cracks the seal faster. Restoring Bellwater and reaching the Chapel become one race, and the player has to choose what the Fenlight is for.

## Themes

The central question: **what should we keep, what should we let go, and who pays for the forgetting?**

Every major character holds a different answer. The town's comfort was built on pushing grief onto someone else. The story asks whether a community can carry its own memories instead.

Tone: cozy on the surface, melancholy underneath. The town is warm, the bread is good, and almost everyone is missing something they can't name. It's a dark fantasy of sorts. Sort of Stardew Valley meets Cult of the Lamb.&#32;

## Factions and foes

**The Lamplighters (****I****sa****b****e****l****):** the old order that tended the lanterns, disbanded in shame. Their shame is double. Publicly, they failed on the night of the theft. Privately, they knew the lanterns were failing and hid it to protect their standing. Linnet learned the truth from their own records.

**The Drowned Court:** a sunken fae nobility under the deep fen, half-swallowed by the Murk. For generations they have carried the town's forgetting (see The world). The Court is split over what to do about it, and not all of them are enemies.

- **Regent Hemlock** (key antagonist): rules the Court in place of a monarch who drowned long ago. He has watched his people dissolve into bog-wights for centuries, and wants to invert the bargain: push the Murk up onto Bellwater and let the folk carry it for once. He isn't cruel for its own sake; he is a father counting his dead. To him, the failing lanterns were the Court's one chance at relief, and Linnet's seal took it away. He knows the player helped. He can be redeemed in only one ending, the Honest Light.
- **Cress** (key protagonist): Hemlock's daughter and heir, sent to Bellwater as an envoy. She believes the burden should be shared, not reversed, and that folk and fae could truly live side by side. She becomes the player's closest Court ally, and the one who makes the Honest Light ending possible. Her arc is choosing between her father and her own convictions. If welcomed, she and Wren fall for each other (see Wren and Cress).
- **Court residents:** some drowned fae climb up to live in town (see Main character arcs). They aren't soldiers or spies. Most just want to remember their own names. Not every folk resident welcomes them.

**The Murkbound:** creatures made of forgotten things. These are the encounter enemies.

- **Bog-wights:** what is left of drowned fae who forgot their own names.
- **Gloam-moths:** moths that flew into the Murk and lost themselves. Some were Sorrel's kin.
- **Reed-wraiths:** forgotten songs and stories, half-heard in the wind.

Defeating or calming a Murkbound releases a **memory fragment**: a lost song, a recipe, a name. Fragments feed the lore and can return things to the town. A late-game option to *remember* enemies rather than destroy them would set encounters apart.

## Expanding into the fen

Pushing light outward pushes forgetting downward. When the town expands into fog-covered land, the Murk recedes and what it hid comes up: lost roads, the sunken orchard, and drowned fae settlements. Every new lantern also adds strain to Linnet's seal.

**Fae settlements.** Some regions hold Court settlements. When the town's territory reaches one, the player chooses how to light it:

| Choice | Town effect | Fae effect | Court Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claim | Full region bonus and resources | The settlement's fae are pushed deeper and drown faster | Falls sharply; Hemlock's faction grows |
| Share | Smaller bonus; the town takes on some forgetting (a resident briefly loses a memory, or a shop slows) | The settlement stays; some of its fae move into town | Rises; Cress's faction grows |
| Leave | No expansion here for now | Unchanged | Unchanged |

**How the fae react** depends on Court Standing:

- **Low:** settlements barricade, Murkbound attacks grow, and Hemlock sends raiders to snuff out town lanterns.
- **Middling:** wary trade and a few residents.
- **High:** settlements invite the player in, trade in memory fragments, and send residents to town.

**Example settlements:**

- **The Silt Market,** off the Old Causeway: a trading town where fae barter in memories.
- **The Pale Weir:** a garrison loyal to Hemlock, guarding the way to the Court. It can only be Claimed or Left.
- **The Willowdeep Hospice:** where fae near dissolution go to be remembered by someone before they become Murkbound.

## Chapters

**1. Rekindling** (exists, rewritten with scenes): the player surfaces at the edge of town with no memories. Wren's last light flares at them, then begins to dim on a rhythm. Wren takes them in and talks them into staying. The town wakes as lanterns are relit. Isabel finds the Thief's scorch trail, which burns an odd colour that Wren recognises and says nothing about. Seed: the Thief's route skipped exactly one house, the Alders'. The player's first memory fragment: a hand in theirs in the dark, and a girl asking if they're nearly there. No major branches; Seal Strain starts rising quietly.

**2. Into the Deep Fen:** expeditions open. Recover the lantern caches and realise they are a trail meant for someone. Learn Isabel's failure on the night of the theft, and hints of the Lamplighters' cover-up through Bodkin's missing page. Old Bramble's maps (some of them wrong), the first Murkbound and memory fragments, and Mother Sedge's warning: every lantern relit in town takes from something holding the deep fen together. The first fae settlement comes into reach, and Cress arrives as the Court's envoy.

**3. Lanterns in the Water:** the Drowned Court and the cost of the bargain. Hemlock reveals that the player was Linnet's guide. Wren, confronted, confesses that the plan was hers and names her sister. Hemlock makes his offer: give the Court the town's light, and he will open the seal and free Linnet. Ends on the Chapel doors, with Linnet's voice on the other side telling Wren to go home (a cliffhanger for chapter 4).

**4. The Drowned Chapel** (planned): Wren, the player and Linnet face each other. Linnet believes the seal is the only answer, and she remembers the player when Wren doesn't. The climax is a confrontation between sisters, not an extraction. Which endings the player can choose here depends on everything before.

## How the story branches

The story branches through three tracked values, a few key choices, and one offer. Chapters 1–3 always play in order; the choices inside them decide which endings are open at the Chapel.

&#91;embedded content: story branches · 4 chapters, 6 endings\]

Seal Strain can end any chapter in the Drowning, and accepting Hemlock's offer skips the Chapel. Which of the four Chapel endings are open depends on Court Standing, Kindling and earlier choices.

**Tracked values**

- **Seal Strain:** rises with every lantern lit in town and every region Claimed; eases when light is banked for the Chapel. It's the clock. The player sees it only through Wren's last light, which dims faster as it rises. If it maxes out, the seal breaks.
- **Court Standing:** rises with Share choices, friendship with fae residents and siding with Cress; falls with Claim choices and turning fae away.
- **Kindling:** the total backstory scenes unlocked across all residents, folk and fae. These are the memories the town could give to a new light.

**Key choices**

| Chapter | Choice | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | First fae settlement: Claim, Share or Leave | Court Standing, and how the Court first sees the town |
| 2 | Welcome Cress as envoy, or send her away | Required for the Honest Light. Turned away, she returns only as Hemlock's messenger |
| 2–3 | Give Bodkin's page to Isabel, or keep it | Exposing the cover-up is required for the Open Fen. Turns Isabel's arc toward redemption or revenge |
| 3 | Keep relighting town, or bank light for the Chapel | Seal Strain against town growth |
| 3 | Hemlock's offer | Accept: the Inverted Bargain, skipping chapter 4. Refuse: on to the Chapel |
| 4 | The final choice at the Chapel | Picks among whichever endings are open |

## Endings and cycles

An idle sim shouldn't take the player's town away as a punishment, so endings work in two layers.

1. **The ending transforms this Bellwater.** Reaching an ending plays an epilogue and permanently changes this save's town: its look, which residents stay, and its production modifiers. The game keeps running as an idle postgame in that form.
2. **The player can let the fen forget.** After any ending, the player can start a new cycle (prestige). The Murk rises, the town forgets, and Bellwater starts over. Because the player is Surfaced, they remember, and keep:
   - **Remembrances:** permanent perks bought with memory fragments, such as faster production, starting resources or extra expedition slots.
   - **Knowledge:** new dialogue options from what they learned, such as telling Wren early that they know about Linnet.
   - **Ledger progress:** endings reached and fragments found.

**What a new cycle is.** The Bellwater story starts over from chapter 1. The player surfaces again, Linnet is back in the seal, and everyone forgets the last cycle. It's a familiar convention for a new run, and the lore explains why the player is the exception.

**Route-locked fragments.** Some of the player's memory fragments appear only on specific routes: one only in the Inverted Bargain, one only in the Open Fen, and so on. No single run can collect them all, so the Keeper naturally takes several cycles without an arbitrary run counter.

Residents don't remember earlier cycles, but a few notice. Mother Sedge tells the player they've done this before. Hemlock remembers outright, because he has spent centuries in the Murk too. He is the only character who knows the player across cycles.

**The Drowning** is the one ending with no postgame. It forces a new cycle, which keeps Seal Strain a real threat.

**Achievements and the Lantern Ledger.** Each ending, chapter and key reveal (Linnet's name, the player's identity, the cover-up) earns an achievement. An in-game Lantern Ledger shows the endings reached, with requirements hidden until each is found.

## Main character arcs (friendship backstories and personal quests)

- **Wren:** the only one who remembers Linnet. The plan to steal the lanterns was hers, and Linnet went in her place. She takes the player in on the first night and works hard to make them stay, because she believes they can help her reach Linnet. Her arc moves from hiding the truth to confessing it. Her answer to the theme: *I kept the memory, but not the courage.*
- **Isabel:** the Lamplighter who failed that night. She may have recognised the Thief and let her go. She also knew about the cover-up and stayed silent. Her arc is choosing whether her anger belongs to the Thief or to herself.
- **Old Bramble:** an old man who remembers the fen before the Fenlight, much older than folk should be. Unlike Mother Sedge, he didn't make the bargain; he only witnessed it, and he misremembers it. He is the cartographer of the deep fen, but his maps are of a fen that partly no longer exists. Remembering a lost place with him can reopen it on the map. He romanticises the old days, and the truth was harder.
- **Marigold:** her family is alive, but **they don't remember the bakery, or her.** The Murk took it from them the night the light went out. Rebuilding the bakery is her attempt to bring their memory back through smell, recipes and routine.
- **Sorrel:** an androgynous moth-fae (they/them) who fears the dark more than anyone. Moths that fly into the Murk become gloam-moths, the enemies the player fights. Some were their kin. They aren't afraid of the dark in general; they're afraid of becoming one of them.
- **Mother Sedge:** she wove the first Fenlight and made the bargain that sank the Court's burden. She knows what the light really is, and withholds it out of shame rather than mystery.
- **Bodkin:** a young Lamplighter descendant, nephew of the Lamplighter who ordered the cover-up. He holds the missing page that proves it. He and Isabel are rivals: she wants the order's failure buried with her, and he wants his uncle's name protected. His arc is choosing between his family's name and the truth. Whether he gives up the page shapes Isabel's arc and opens the Open Fen.

**Shorter arcs.** Each holds one position on the theme:

- **Pip:** a young man who wants to forget something on purpose, and asks the player to help.
- **Dunstan:** the town archivist, who hoards memories obsessively: keepsakes, notes, lists of names. He is terrified the Murk will take more. His arc is learning that a memory kept alone isn't the same as one shared.
- **Hazel:** doesn't remember her own past and is content without it.

**Drowned Court residents.** They arrive as Court Standing rises. Each is also a source of Kindling, and some folk residents resent them at first.

- **Cress:** Hemlock's daughter and envoy (see Factions). She becomes a resident only if welcomed in chapter 2, and then has full hearts and a personal quest. Her quest decides whether she breaks with her father openly, and whether she can bring her father back.
- **Pennywort:** a cheerful fae trader who forgot her own name and picked "Pennywort" out of a jar. She runs a memory-fragment stall. Her arc: deciding whether she wants her real name back once she finds it.
- **Old Eelgrass:** an ancient courtier, nearly Murkbound, who comes to town to spend his last season being remembered by someone. His arc: the player helps record his memories before he dissolves, or saves him in the Honest Light.
- **Bulrush:** a deserter from Hemlock's garrison at the Pale Weir. Isabel distrusts him on sight. Depending on Court Standing, he becomes a loyal guard, a double agent, or Hemlock's spy all along.

### Wren and Cress

If the player welcomes Cress, a romance grows between her and Wren. It runs on Cress's hearts, and the player can nudge it along or leave it as a friendship. It mirrors the theme: love as a memory freely given.

1. **Chapter 2, the envoy arrives.** Wren is the only resident who refuses to meet her. Her distrust is a cover: she is afraid the Court knows about Linnet.
2. **Cress, heart 2.** Cress notices that Wren's lantern dims like breathing. She recognises a light tied to someone in the Murk, and says nothing. They begin keeping each other's secrets.
3. **Cress, heart 3 (quest start).** Cress asks Wren to help carve her Court mask, so she will remember her own face. Wren hasn't carved a mask since the one Linnet wore that night. The player chooses whether to encourage Wren to say how she feels, or keep it a friendship.
4. **Chapter 3, after the confession.** When the town turns on Wren, Cress is the one who stays. She is the first person to ask what Linnet was like.
5. **Cress, heart 5.** Wren gives Cress a memory of Linnet to keep, so that if the Murk takes it from Wren, someone will still have it.

**How it ends:**

- **The Honest Light:** together. They lead the folk and fae districts side by side, the phrase finally true.
- **The Open Fen:** together in the fog, where nobody forgets anything.
- **The Old Light:** Cress must return to a Court that still carries the burden. They write by heron; bittersweet.
- **The Inverted Bargain:** the fog makes the folk slowly forget. Wren forgets Cress; Cress remembers everything.
- **The Keeper:** together if Court Standing is high; otherwise Cress goes home.
- **The Drowning:** both are lost.

### Hemlock's redemption

Hemlock can be redeemed only in the Honest Light, and only if Cress's personal quest is complete. In the final scene he gives his hoarded Fenlight scraps, and his own memories of the Court's dead, to the new light, so they are carried by everyone instead of by him alone. In every other ending he stays an enemy: swearing to return in the Old Light, ruling as the thing he hated in the Inverted Bargain, or diminished and alone in the Open Fen.

## Milestones

### M1: Story scenes and friendship (the foundation)

**Dialogue scenes** (`scenes.json`): ordered lines of `{speaker, text, mood}` with optional choices (each choice can change friendship). Triggers: chapter start, quest start/complete, resident arrival, friendship level, expedition result (used later). A full-screen SceneView with portraits on left and right, the speaker's name, tap to advance, and a Skip button. Scenes play once and can be replayed from the character page.

**Friendship** (0–5 hearts per resident), raised by: working at a shop (a little per collect), their quests, gifts (M2), and expeditions together (M3). Each heart unlocks a backstory scene. Heart 3 starts their personal quest line; heart 5 gives a perk (e.g. +20% at their favourite shop).

**Lore hook: backstory scenes are memories.** In story terms, each heart is a resident trusting Wren with a memory. In the final chapter, these are the memories a resident can choose to give to a new Fenlight. Deeper friendships give the honest-light ending more to burn on. The data can be as simple as a count of unlocked backstory scenes per resident.

**Character page** (from the resident panel and Townsfolk): portrait, hearts, likes, backstory scenes to replay, and their quest line.

**Chapters:** quests get chapter structure with a chapter title card. Chapter 1 is rewritten with scenes; chapter 2's opening is written, but its gameplay waits for M3.

- Set Seal Strain, Court Standing and Kindling directly.
- Toggle each story flag: Cress welcomed, cover-up exposed, Hemlock's offer accepted, settlement choices.
- Set any resident's hearts.
- Trigger any ending and its epilogue, and preview the postgame town form.

- Save and load named presets, one per ending, e.g. "Honest Light, one step before the Chapel".

## Clues to seed and open questions

**Clues to plant early** so players can suspect the twists before they land:

- The scorch trail burns an unusual colour that matches Wren's last light.
- The Thief's mask is something Wren made as a child; a matching one hangs in her house.
- The Thief's route skipped exactly one house, the Alders'.
- Nobody in town mentions a second Alder child, but there are two beds in Wren's loft.
- Mother Sedge flinches whenever someone thanks the Fenlight.
- Wren's last light flares around the player, and only the player.
- The player can read the code in Linnet's caches without knowing why.
- Hemlock calls the player by a name they don't recognise.
